How it works

Four steps from a card to a set of keys.

room8 matches you to a household, then keeps the whole application in one place instead of nine chat threads and a shared folder.

01

Swipe on a home

A card is a room, a warm rent and a doorbell panel of the people already living there. A dashed square is an open spot.

02

Ask to join

Interest goes to the household, not to one person. They answer as a group, so nobody is left guessing.

03

Agree the house rules

Rota, quiet hours, guests, smoking. Everyone signs off before a single document leaves your phone.

04

Apply together

One readiness bar shows what the landlord is still missing. Viewings and answers land in In progress.

Inside the app

Five screens, and nothing that a hairline could not do instead.

Real screenshots from the build. Green carries structure, brass appears once per screen, and the room does the emotional work.

The card

The room does the talking.

Photo first, then the facts in the order you actually ask for them: where, when, how much, who, and why this household thinks you fit.

  • {{TICK}}Doorbell panel. Members sit over the photo as square tiles, one dashed square per open spot.
  • {{TICK}}Warm rent, tabular. One brass underline per screen — the only decoration on the card.
  • {{TICK}}Habits, not hashtags. Tidy-ish, quiet weekdays, guests welcome. Terracotta marks the ones you share.

Groups

The group is the unit, not the room.

Every household you are part of, applying to, or still deciding about, in one list — with the money and the open spots visible at a glance.

  • {{TICK}}Live status. Searching, applying, one spot left, housed in June.
  • {{TICK}}Faces, not counts. You always know who is in the room before you tap in.
  • {{TICK}}Start your own. Two friends and an empty spot is already a household on room8.

Group detail

The awkward conversation, already had.

The house agreement is a screen, not a hope. Requests to join get a single Answer button, and the readiness bar names exactly what is missing.

  • {{TICK}}House agreement. Rota, quiet after 22:00, guests with a heads-up — agreed by all three.
  • {{TICK}}Application readiness. 4 of 6, and it tells you whose income proof is still outstanding.
  • {{TICK}}One chat per household. Not one chat per person you swiped.

In progress

Everything in flight, on one page.

Applications, conversations and viewings stop living in your inbox. Sent, answered, invited, expired — each one says what happens next.

  • {{TICK}}Viewing invited. The one brass moment on the screen, with the day and the time.
  • {{TICK}}Honest about silence. “No answer in 14 days” is a state, and it expires cleanly.
  • {{TICK}}Latest messages. The last line from each household, nothing more.

Profile

Your papers stay yours.

Preferences drive matching. Documents sit behind a lock and only travel inside an application you have approved — and you can take all of it back out again.

  • {{TICK}}Documents — private. 3 of 5, missing proof of income and a landlord reference.
  • {{TICK}}Verified, quietly. A green check, an age, a job. No score, no ranking.
  • {{TICK}}Export and delete. Both are one tap from the bottom of your own profile.

Design system

Warm paper, one brass key, no gradients.

Fraunces 600 for anything with a name, DM Sans for everything else. Green is structure and navigation only; brass is allowed exactly one genuine moment per screen.

Residential vocabulary

“Two spots left in Neukölln,” never “New match nearby!” Nothing in the product borrows from dating apps.

Flat paper, warm shadow

No gradients anywhere. A single soft shadow lifts the card off the plaster, and hairlines do the rest.

One brass moment

The rent underline, the spot-left badge, or the viewing invite — one per screen, never two.
